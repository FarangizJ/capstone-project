1 Data Overview & Quality

The master dataset covers 34 confirmed years (1990–2023) plus 3 preliminary rows (2024–2026). Data is drawn from IEA, IRENA, and World Bank — all tier-1 sources. Key completeness issues are concentrated in solar/wind (only measurable post-2017) and capacity data.

2 Historical Trends (1990–2023)

Uzbekistan's power sector is dominated by natural gas. Hydropower is the only significant renewable source. Solar and wind generation only began appearing in the data from 2021. RE penetration has not improved structurally since the 1990s.

Electricity Generation by Source (TWh)

Natural gas has dominated since independence. Coal grew sharply post-2018. Solar/wind is only visible from 2021 onward.

RE Penetration vs. Fossil Share (%)

RE peaked at 18.7% in 2005 (high-hydro year) then structurally declined. By 2020 it fell to 7.5% — the lowest since independence.

CO&sub2; Intensity of Power Generation (gCO&sub2;/kWh)

CO&sub2; intensity fluctuates with hydro availability. The 2018–2020 dip reflects a methodological shift, not a structural improvement.

Electricity Demand vs. GDP Growth

Demand contracted after independence (1991–2005) as GDP collapsed, then rebounded. The 2021 spike reflects post-COVID rebound and data revision.

Generation Mix — Share by Source (%)

Gas share remains 75–85% across the entire period. Coal share nearly doubled 2018–2023. Hydro fluctuates seasonally. Solar/wind share is barely visible (<1%).

3 Model Performance

Four forecasting targets were modelled using both statistical (ARIMA) and machine learning (Ridge, Lasso, Gradient Boosting) approaches. The merged notebook selected the best model per target based on test MAPE and cross-validated MAPE.

Important caveat — CO&sub2; Intensity model The best CO&sub2; intensity model (Ridge) has R² = −0.48, meaning it performs worse than predicting the historical mean. CO&sub2; intensity forecasts should be treated as directional estimates only, not point predictions.

Electricity Demand Forecast (TWh)

All scenarios show strong demand growth driven by population (+35M) and GDP expansion. Accelerated transition adds efficiency investments, slightly raising demand.

Renewable Energy Penetration Forecast (%)

Baseline RE stays flat at ~11.7% through 2040 — essentially no progress. Accelerated reaches 28.7% by 2040. Delayed falls to 3.2% as fossil capacity expands faster.

Fossil Generation Forecast (TWh)

Fossil generation grows under all scenarios due to rising demand. The gap between Accelerated and Delayed by 2040 is ~21 TWh — equivalent to ~3 large gas plants.

CO&sub2; Intensity Forecast (gCO&sub2;/kWh) — Directional

Shown as directional estimate only (R²=−0.48). Accelerated scenario indicates a ~40% reduction in CO&sub2; intensity by 2040 if RE targets are met.

5 Key Findings & Recommendations

Summary of the most important conclusions from the data analysis and modelling exercise.

Structural Fossil Lock-in

Uzbekistan is ~90% fossil-dependent as of 2023. RE penetration has not improved since the 1990s — the 2005 peak of 18.7% was driven by hydro, not by new capacity. There is no structural transition underway.

Demand Growing Fast

Electricity demand grew from 39 TWh (2005) to 74 TWh (2023) — nearly doubling in 18 years. Ridge regression forecasts demand reaching 100–123 TWh by 2030, with MAPE=3.2%.

Only Accelerated Scenario Breaks the Trend

The Baseline scenario keeps RE flat at 11.7% through 2040. Only the Accelerated scenario — requiring sustained policy intervention — achieves RE above 25% by 2035.

Solar/Wind Data Gap

Solar data is 81% missing historically; wind is 92% missing. This limits RE modelling accuracy. Post-2021 data suggests rapid ramp-up is starting but remains small (<1% share).

Demand Model is Reliable

The Ridge regression demand model achieves R²=0.82 and MAPE=3.2% — robust enough for planning purposes. The ARIMA fossil generation model (MAPE=3.0%) is equally solid.

CO&sub2; Intensity Model Unreliable

R²=−0.48 means the model is worse than predicting the mean. Use the CO&sub2; scenario results as directional estimates only — wide confidence intervals should be communicated to stakeholders.
