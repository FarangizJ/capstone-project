How to read this map. Status is shown by fill: solid = operational, medium = under construction or contracted, faint dashed = planned. The timeline shows each asset from its commissioning (or target) year, reproducing the sector's chronological expansion. Soviet-era thermal and hydropower were state-built with no disclosed project financials; transition-era plants were state-owned but development-bank financed; the 2019-onward renewables and storage are privately built independent power projects (IPPs) under 25-year power-purchase agreements with the state as guaranteed off-taker.

Coordinate accuracy. Markers flagged “exact” use published plant or site coordinates (the six legacy thermal stations, the Gazli gas field, the Jizzakh nuclear site). Renewable, storage and small-hydro markers are placed at their host district town and are approximate. The national border is a simplified outline; this is a schematic reference map, not a survey-grade GIS layer.

Profit note. Net profit is not publicly disclosed for any project; the “returns” field shows the contracted PPA tariff where known. Uzbek tariff regulation caps the allowed margin at 10–20%.

Decommissioning. No Soviet- or transition-era plant has closed; all still operate. Aging units inside them are being progressively retired (Tashkent, Tahiatash, Angren) or were left unfinished (Novo-Angren Unit 8), against a national target to shut about 6.4 GW of morally and physically outdated thermal units. Plants carrying retired or idle capacity are marked with a hatched ring.

Primary sources — IEA Uzbekistan energy profile; EES EAEC; ADB, EBRD, AIIB and IFC project documents; ACWA Power and Masdar project pages; PwC Uzbekistan tax summaries; UNCTAD Investment Policy Monitor; World Nuclear News. Border outline: glynnbird/countriesgeojson (simplified).
